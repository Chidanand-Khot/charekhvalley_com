Can Outsiders Buy Land in Uttarakhand? Rules for Non-Residents

A complete legal breakdown of Uttarakhand's land regulations, residential size caps, agricultural exemptions, and safe purchasing procedures for prospective investors.

Home / Blog / Land Rules Guide • Published: October 26, 2026 • 9 Min Read

Uttarakhand—cherished as Devbhumi—attracts countless families and investors yearning for a serene holiday home or retirement haven in the Himalayas. However, navigating real estate acquisition in the state requires a precise understanding of regional property laws. If you are a non-resident asking can outsiders buy land in Uttarakhand, the answer involves specific statutory limits, district-by-district classifications, and procedural compliance.

Key Legal Principle to Remember

Uttarakhand land regulations distinguish rigorously between land categories (residential vs. agricultural) and domicile status. Ensuring accurate land classification before signing agreements is critical to maintaining compliance under state laws.

1. Residential Land Rules & the 250 Sq M Cap

For individuals looking to build an independent holiday cottage or residential villa, non-residents from outside Uttarakhand are permitted to buy residential land. However, under state property regulations, there is a clear size threshold:

  • The 250 Square Meter Limit: An individual buyer (including spouse, minor children, and dependent parents as a single unit) can purchase up to 250 square meters—roughly 2,690 square feet or 300 square yards—of residential land.
  • One-Time Purchase Affidavit: Buyers must submit a sworn legal affidavit confirming that they have not previously utilized this residential land quota elsewhere in the state.

2. Agricultural and Horticultural Land Restrictions

A common point of confusion among buyers is whether they can purchase large agricultural plots or farmlands in the hills. The legal reality is strict:

Outsiders are legally barred from purchasing agricultural or horticultural land in 11 out of Uttarakhand's 13 hill districts (including popular regions like Dehradun, Nainital, Pauri Garhwal, Almora, and Tehri Garhwal). Agricultural land transactions for non-domiciles are restricted strictly, with rare exceptions only in plains districts like Haridwar and Udham Singh Nagar, which require rigorous state government approvals rather than routine local registration.
